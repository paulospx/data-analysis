What this workspace is for

The file lists paid circulation — average paid copies per issue, not unique subscribers. Digital and print are separate channels. A daily newspaper and a monthly magazine are not time-equivalent; keep that in mind when you sum across titles, or switch on frequency weighting.

Statistical charts

  • Stacked quarter bars — market level, so you can see whether digital is substituting print or sitting beside it.
  • Ranking bars — comparison of titles on a shared scale (the primary analytical task).
  • Log scatter — print vs digital. Log scale is required: titles range from hundreds to ~100k copies.
  • Beeswarm of digital share — the distribution, not just the average. Averages hide print-only clusters.
  • Slope chart Q1→Q4 — two-period change without the clutter of four-point lines.
  • Diverging change bars — signed percent change, ranked by magnitude.
  • Waterfall — which titles moved the market total between two quarters.
  • Sparklines in the table — lookup plus trend in the same row (details on demand).

Interaction (NN/g)

  • Overview, filter, then details — KPIs first, then charts, then the table.
  • Filters stay visible — you should never have to remember what is applied.
  • Non-reporting titles are off by default — zeros are often missing filings, not true zero circulation.
  • Irregular titles (quarterly, bimonthly) are averaged only over quarters that report copies.
  • Click a title to pin it. Pins follow you across views.
  • Color is reserved for meaning: blue = digital, terracotta = print, green/red = change.
  • Chart titles state the finding, not the chart type. They update with your filters.
